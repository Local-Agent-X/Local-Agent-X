# Pitch deck playbook

**Audience:** investors deciding in minutes whether to take a meeting. They skim; every slide gets one idea.

**Story, in order (10–14 slides):** cover → problem (`statement`) → solution (`content` with a product image) → product (`content` or `image`) → why now → market (`stats`, real numbers with a source) → business model (`columns` for tiers or pricing) → traction (`stats` or a chart; never a placeholder) → competition (`columns`: them vs. us) → go-to-market (`timeline`) → team (photos and one line each) → the ask (`closing`: the amount and what it buys).

**Text:** a title that states the point ("Private AI is a $113B market by 2034", not "Market"). At most 40 words and 4 bullets a slide; the rest goes in speaker notes. Numbers big, sources small.

**Cover:** product name large, a one-line value proposition, founder name and contact under it. Use the cover's image panel for the product or its setting.

**Design:** midnight, signal or harbor; the founder's brand if they have one.

**Imagery:** the real product beats stock. Never a generic handshake or a stock "team" photo standing in for the real team.

**Leave out:** anything you could not defend in the meeting: invented traction, unsourced market numbers, "[ADD: …]" placeholders on a slide (put open questions in your reply, not the deck).

**Review the rendered deck:** Is the cover strong enough to open a meeting? Does each slide make exactly one point, readable in three seconds? Is the ask unmistakable? Do the layouts vary, with numbers shown as stats or charts rather than bullets?
